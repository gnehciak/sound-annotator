Sound Annotator

Privacy Policy

Last updated 7 September 2026

Sound Annotator is a classroom tool for making timestamped notes on music. This page describes exactly what it stores, why, and who else can see it. It is short because the app collects very little.

What we collect when you sign in

Signing in uses Sign in with Google. We ask Google for the openid, email and profile scopes and nothing else, and we receive only:

  • your Google account's unique identifier;
  • your email address;
  • your name and profile picture URL.

We never receive or store your Google password, and we have no access to your Gmail, Drive, Calendar, contacts, or any other Google service. Your email address identifies your account and is how we recognise the administrator; your name is shown as the author on tracks you choose to publish publicly.

What we store when you use the app

  • Your tracks — the title, the link to the video or audio you are annotating, your notes, and your per-track settings.
  • Images you put in notes — these are the only files we host. They are stored at unguessable URLs.
  • A session cookie — one cookie that keeps you signed in. It is not used for advertising or tracking, and there are no third-party analytics, advertising, or tracking scripts anywhere in this app.
  • A one-way hash of your IP address, only if you create a track without an account, and only to limit how many can be created from one network. The address itself is never stored.

Audio is never uploaded. A track points at a link — a YouTube video, a Google Drive file, or an audio URL — and your browser streams it from wherever it already lives.

Who can see your work

Your tracks are private to your account by default. They become visible to other people only when you choose it: by creating a share link, or by publishing a track to the public gallery. The site administrator can see all tracks in order to operate and moderate the service.

Who processes data for us

  • Google — sign-in, and video playback when a track points at YouTube or Drive.
  • Vercel — hosting, and storage of note images.
  • Neon — the database holding accounts and tracks.
  • Replicate — an administrator-only feature that analyses a track's audio to detect its musical sections.

We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone else.

Keeping and deleting data

Deleting a track moves it to a trash, where you can restore it. Anything left in the trash is permanently deleted, along with its images, after 30 days. To delete your account and everything in it, email the address below and we will remove it.

Children

The app is designed so students do not need accounts: a student can open a link, annotate, and hand work back without ever signing in or giving us a name or an email address. Accounts are intended for teachers.
